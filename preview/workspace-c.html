    How C is organised: queues of tickets

    The left column is the help-desk queue. Queues climb in tiers: Tier 1 Home, Tier 2 Small office, Tier 3 Small business, Tier 4 Campus, Tier 5 Enterprise. Each ticket picks a network template sized for its objective and generates its fault, so students can't memorise them. The Network-Sims become ticket sets in the tier that fits (the sim plus five more). Extra training and Exam Practice sit beside the queue.

    What it does well

    • The strongest Rafiki's IT Services story: you're the tech, Mason assigns the work
    • Every ticket is evidence first: commands, lights, notebook, then the diagnosis
    • The physical view (the house, the office) makes cable and Wi-Fi faults real
    • Fits the program's six-option, red-stays-red diagnosis exactly

    What it gives up

    • No fixed networks to explore for their own sake
    • Build mode feels like a ticket too (a work order), not a free sandbox
    • Moving between sites only happens when a ticket spans them
